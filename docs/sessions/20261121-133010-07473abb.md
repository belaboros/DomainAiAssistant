## 3 · 13:41 UTC

### Request

> Please add the explanation to the key why the paragraph with Cicero's quote shall be left out from the ontology.

### Response

I added a paragraph to the answer key, [docs/research/notes/pet-manuals-corpus/README.md](docs/research/notes/pet-manuals-corpus/README.md), explaining why the Cicero passage stays out of the ontology. It sits right after the table of passages to exclude.

The reasons, in short:

1. **It's not a rule.** A rule in the ontology says who must do what, and when. The passage names no actor, action, condition or limit, so nothing can be checked against it.
2. **It's too vague to apply.** "In most cases" doesn't say which cases. As a rule it would overlap with every specific rule, or seem to override it. It would also show up as a false contradiction wherever a rule protects what one resident is allowed to do.
3. **The quote is about something else.** Cicero was writing about the laws of a state, not these buildings. It lends the rules authority but defines nothing in this domain.
4. **The concrete rules already say it.** Quiet hours, the shared yard and clear escape routes are where the common interest actually becomes something. The ontology captures those rules, not the motive behind them.
5. **It wouldn't help a newcomer.** A level-1 concept should show what this domain is about, and "live in harmony" fits any community.

It closes with a fallback: if a reviewer wants to keep the intent, it can go in a description of the ontology itself (for example `dcterms:description` or a `skos:scopeNote`), not in a concept, relation or rule.

None of this is committed yet. It can go to the feature branch along with the manual edits.
